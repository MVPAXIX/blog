Most managers will opt to have quick meetings and quick replies in Slack. In fact, pinging and status calls that are back to back destroy engineering momentum. An async-first culture isn't a time zone concept, it's a more respectful approach to deep focus and a high level of clarity and structured communication, rather than noise.

{/* truncate */}

## Default to Public, Written Documentation

Project Stagnation happens when info is in their brain or in a private DM, when they go offline, or walk into a meeting.

* **When making big feature decisions, write RFCs instead of holding a meeting:** Technical design document or Request for Comments (RFCs). Do not need 45 minutes of alignment sync that is asynchronously driven, but give the team 24 hours to look at and comment on.
* **Tickets should be specific:** Should not be a title like "Fix login bug." Should be very clearly outlined and have technical constraints such that any engineer should be able to reproduce and solve without having to ask 3 follow up questions to determine what to do.

## Honour Asynchronous SLAs for the protection of Deep Work

Excessive notifications are the gravest obstacle to developer flow. Let's say an engineer expects a response to a Slack message within 5 mins, then they could no longer wait for 3 continuous hours for the engineer to write complicated codes.

* **Determine Urgency Classes:** Establish response time expectations. Use of a phone call or emergency alert shall only be used when there is a true production outage. For all other PR review activities, non-critical questions, design changes, set reasonable time limits (4-8 hours) to protect deep work within distributed engineering teams.
* **Batch communication:** Engineers will communicate at regular intervals (e.g. at the start and end of the day) via Slack or email, rather than opening it throughout the day.

## From Presence to Deliverables

Conventional office spaces can often be mistaken for being productive with visibility. In an async culture, there's no true difference between active vs passive; whether a status bubble shows up or metrics are collected for mouse movement is moot.

* **Measure sprints:** Focus on what's being accomplished - closed pull requests, closed tickets, reliable deployment etc.
* **Let them do their own job:** They should be left alone, and set boundaries so they can be allowed to get over the finishing line without being "hand-held" by developers, fostering a healthy remote work culture.
