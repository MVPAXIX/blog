# The Memory Leak on Tuesdays: An Engineering War Story

It began as a little blip. Our primary microservice that's built with Node.js will have a memory spike every Tuesday at 9:00 AM UTC. The containers will reach their limits and will crash with an Out of Memory (OOM) error at 11:00 AM and restart.

In the realm of Offshore Software Development, when a bug shows up once a week, it's a tough battle of stamina. Our Distributed Engineering Teams were unable to understand. There hadn't been a significant update to the code in two weeks, traffic was as expected, and CPU was flat. But the servers would lie in wait on every Tuesday and slowly and quietly snap and die.

We played off the beaten path. Was it a bad cron job? No. Was it lock of the tables in the databases on a weekly basis? No.

This is where Remote Team Management comes into play. Engineers were waiting around the world for Tuesday morning to come by, waiting to stare at DataDog dashboards all over the continents. We've developed a Remote Work Culture based on trust and autonomy, and this thing was causing a lot of friction. It was the Asynchronous Work Strategies that it threw off balance because everyone was suddenly compelled to be online synchronously in order to see the perpetrator in real-time.

It threatened our Remote Talent Retention, too – our lead backend engineer spent three back-to-back weekends developing custom memory profiling tools instead of taking a break. In fact, during Remote Developer Onboarding, we began joking that Tuesdays were cursed. For our front-line Distributed Agile Teams, however, it wasn't funny any more. The clients' trust was being eroded because of the erratic downtimes.

In the fourth week a breakthrough was made. It was a huge success of Cross-Cultural Team Integration. We recently recruited a developer from our Borderless Hiring program that detected a bizarre jump in a report in a legacy reporting module.

Once a week, an automated script was producing weekly compliance reports to meet International Tech Compliance standards for one of our FinTech clients every Tuesday morning. The legacy script was performing read operations on the database to pull 500,000 rows into memory at once to create the CSV.

However, why just on Tuesdays?

This was due to a custom weekly snapshot request by a legacy client two years back, and the query had only been written as a global scope, thus pulling all data from all clients to memory each Tuesday even if one client wanted the snapshot.

The data extraction method was changed to the use of streams in Node.js. The size of memory was immediately reduced from 4GB to 40MB without any fluctuations. It was finally Tuesday and the curse had been lifted.
