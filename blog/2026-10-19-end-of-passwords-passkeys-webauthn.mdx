# The End of Passwords: Why Passkeys and WebAuthn are the New Enterprise Standard

If your application depends on a password, then you're taking a risk with your whole business by relying on a shared secret.

Your back end still has to check a secret that a human enters in a browser – regardless of whether you salt and hash the password with bcrypt. Any human can be phished, tricked or keylogged. Organizations that engage in Offshore Software Development should not use passwords as this creates a lot of attack vectors. If one developer in your Distributed Engineering Teams clicks on a clever phishing e-mail, a bad guy gets the keys to your production infrastructure.

This old authentication scheme is a basic undermining of your International Tech Compliance. It's impossible to practice zero-trust architecture and ask users to remember a string of special characters.

## The Cryptographic Reality

WebAuthn and Passkeys completely do away with the shared secret by instead using public key cryptography on the hardware level.

When someone signs up for your app with a Passkey, their device (with the use of a TPM chip or Apple's Secure Enclave) creates a private and public key pair.

- The **Private Key** never gets removed from the physical device of the user. It can't be hacked, copied or leaked from the server database since the server will never have it.
- The **Public Key** is passed onto your database at the back end.

The server sends a cryptographic challenge to log in. The device asks the user for biometric authentication (FaceID, TouchID, Windows Hello). The user provides it, the private key is unlocked, and the signature of the challenge is created and sent back. Phishing attacks are mathematically impossible since there is no password to steal.

## Identity as the Foundation

In addition to the huge security enhancements, the killing of passwords significantly optimizes Remote Team Management.

Passkeys simplify the flow of Asynchronous Work Strategies by eliminating the need for engineers and clients to wait for a helpdesk to reset a forgotten password. This no-friction access improves your Remote Work Culture in an instant. In addition, Remote Developer Onboarding evolves from a painstaking multi-day provisioning of enterprise password managers to a 2-minute hardware provisioning process without any friction.

Once you have the identity layer secured, you eliminate a huge area of worry about operations. This also has a positive impact on Remote Talent Retention, since your best engineers will save their weekends from having to respond to credential compromise events.

## The Deployment Hurdles

WebAuthn isn't merely a new front-end SDK. It demands Distributed Agile Teams to totally redesign their approach to session and cryptographic issues.

It needs a good degree of Cross-Cultural Team Integration to carry out this. The public key storage and the verification of the cryptographic signatures are a job for backend engineers, while frontend engineers undertake the creation of biometric prompt flows in a coordinated manner. The cost of the engineering work, however, is insignificant compared with the long-term security benefit.
