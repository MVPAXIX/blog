# Managing Burnout in Distributed Teams: Spotting the Early Signs Before Remote Workers Disengage

Team burnout in a distributed team is rarely an explosive event. Rather, it's a subtle, step-by-step withdrawal in which high performers slowly lose their verve, no longer participate in conversations, and just fade away. As with any warning sign, it's important for remote managers to be attuned to the changing dynamics in communication, work habits and output, as physical signs of burnout – such as someone staying late at work or appearing tired in the office – won't be present.

## Spotting the Early Warning Signs

When remote team members fall into burnout, they tend to show signs in their daily routine well before their coding quality or their output standards suffer.

- Communication changes: Notice when team members are becoming silent in public Slack groups, not asking questions, or not providing detailed information anymore but just a simple response.
- Activity peaks during off hours: Is it possible that the developers are regularly pushing code, sending messages or opening PRs at 2 AM local time? The quickest way to get tired is to work 24 hours a day and try to "catch up".
- Pay attention during the regular check-ins: Listen with empathy during 1-on-1s; don't get cynical or tired. When you start to become frustrated about any minor technical issues or don't seem to be interested in the next project, these are obvious signs of mental fatigue.

## Realistic strategies for avoiding burn-out

The only thing it is not about is a Friday off – it is about systemic operational habits forcing people to overwork.

- Set clear boundaries around time off: Don't allow time off to be optional, it must be required. Make sure all members of the team are fully disconnected while they take a break by removing the active ticket from the Leave workflow before they log off.
- Avoid pressure on notifications: Make it very clear to your team that notifications that are not urgent do not need to be responded to immediately outside their local working hours. Get async communication going, so that engineers are not bound to Slack.
- Normalize workload adjustments: Weekly 1-on-1s to discuss workload and backlog. Don't expect an engineer to sprint indefinitely with all his priorities, if he is overloaded, actively prune his priorities.

## Measuring Long-Term Impact

Active approach to burnout and the creation of sustainable remote working environment goes far beyond employee wellness.

- Increased retention, decreased turnover: Engineers who are supported and feel rested are more likely to remain with companies longer, which helps to retain domain knowledge and minimizes hiring expenses.
- Stable velocity across sprints: A rested team produces consistent velocity (not sprints with a lot of features followed by a lot of fatigue).
