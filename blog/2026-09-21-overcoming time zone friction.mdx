  <introduction>
    Employing people in distant time zones will almost always play out in one of two ways: Either there is a 24/7, clockwork product-development process, or you are stuck waiting 12 hours for one Slack response[cite: 7]. To achieve the “follow the sun” model, which requires flawless work transition between continents, you need to remove the friction from daily activities[cite: 7].
  </introduction>

  <main_content>
    <section>
      <sub_headline>Stop Relying on Real-Time Meetings</sub_headline>
      <body>A 7hr difference will make your team unproductive if everyone needs to be in the same Zoom meeting to make a decision[cite: 7].</body>
      <list_item>Use Async: All task, feature specs and bug reports must be well documented[cite: 7]. Utilizing effective asynchronous work strategies, the task context should be very clear on the Nairobi ticket if a developer logs on as a developer logs off in San Francisco[cite: 7].</list_item>
      <list_item>Establish and agree on Handoff Protocols: Convene end-of-day updates[cite: 7]. You keep the next time zone moving without delay with a simple three-line summary (What got shipped / What is blocked / What is next.)[cite: 7].</list_item>
    </section>

    <section>
      <sub_headline>Take a Look at Overlap Hours, NOT Full Overlap</sub_headline>
      <body>It is not necessary for team members to work on the night shift to remain connected[cite: 7]. Try to overlap the 2–3-hour window as much as possible for your distributed engineering teams[cite: 7].</body>
      <list_item>That time is only to be used for unblocking, code review and high priority alignment[cite: 7].</list_item>
      <list_item>Limit deep-focus time to the non-overlapping hours[cite: 7].</list_item>
    </section>

    <section>
      <sub_headline>Focus on Output and Not Hours Online</sub_headline>
      <body>There's no better way to mess with a global team than by having to see how their mouse moves; or when they're expected to be constantly replying to status updates[cite: 7].</body>
      <list_item>This may require setting up clear sprint output metrics, but as long as developers feel they are in control and have known expectations, geographic boundaries go out the window, fostering a healthy remote work culture and boosting remote talent retention[cite: 7].</list_item>
    </section>
