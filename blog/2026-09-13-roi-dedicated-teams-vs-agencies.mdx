# The ROI of Dedicated Remote Teams vs. Traditional Project Outsourcing Agencies

When software companies need to grow their engineering power quickly, they typically go towards two options: they either acquire a traditional project-based outsourcing agency, or they create a team of engineers on a remote basis[cite: 7]. On the surface, agencies seem like a convenient choice, but they have inherent friction, high overhead and incentives[cite: 7]. The actual ROI (return on investment) of either model depends on three major factors: context, cost structure and momentum going forward[cite: 7].

## Product Context and Code Quality

The old-fashioned agencies often move developers from one client to another[cite: 7]. Each time another engineer enters your project, you're paying for his or her induction period[cite: 7]. Agency contracts focus more on getting the job done within a designated time frame and scope, which means that long-term architecture and clean code may not be as much of a priority[cite: 7].

- A remote team, on the other hand, is a group of employees that work on your product exclusively[cite: 7]. They become part of your morning routine, show up at your daily standup, and are accountable for maintainability, saving you thousands in technical debt while supporting your distributed engineering teams[cite: 7].

## Understanding Cost Structure and Transparency

The initial impression of “safe” fixed price quotes by agencies is misleading, because the scope of the work changes, the features change and after launch issues occur and it is just a matter of time before additional costs occur[cite: 7]. Moreover, agency billable rates have high management markups[cite: 7].

- If you are using dedicated IT staff augmentation, you will know what your costs are going to be[cite: 7]. No markups for middlemen on each and every billable hour—you pay normal salary and admin overhead[cite: 7].

## Team Continuity and Retention

At a traditional agency you are rarely able to choose who the specific developers are going to work on your project, nor are they known for having a high turnover in the agency[cite: 7]. Frequent transfer of work delays delivery[cite: 7].

- When you have a dedicated team, you knowingly hire each engineer through targeted tech talent acquisition and incorporate him or her into your engineering culture[cite: 7]. With remote developers feeling like part of your company, instead of just a disposable contract, remote talent retention increases and feature output increases[cite: 7].
