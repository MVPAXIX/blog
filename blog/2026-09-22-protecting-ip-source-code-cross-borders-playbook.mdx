  <introduction>
    Securing your code, algorithms, and key technology is as crucial as shipping features when you expand your dev team globally[cite: 7]. It takes a combination of solid contracts and savvy, everyday security practices to get done[cite: 7]. When scaling technical capacity across borders, you cannot rely solely on paper agreements or assume developers understand implicit security expectations[cite: 7]. Building a resilient offshore operation requires an execution-level playbook that pairs enforceable legal frameworks with strict technical guardrails to maintain international tech compliance[cite: 7].
  </introduction>

  <main_content>
    <section>
      <sub_headline>Ensure Contracts Are in Place (Prior to Sharing Access)</sub_headline>
      <body>Legal documents are not enough on their own to stop a bad actor, but they will put in place boundaries and, if there is a problem, the legal documents will give you teeth to bite with[cite: 7]. Do not give access to the repository or system architecture information without signature[cite: 7]:</body>
      <list_item>Strict NDAs: Ensure your NDA specifically includes product roadmaps, database schemas, binaries, and source code[cite: 7]. It must also cover not only the prime agency/contractor, but any other people that they invite onto the project[cite: 7].</list_item>
      <list_item>Work for Hire / Ownership Transfers: An NDA obscures the facts, but does not necessarily mean you own the work[cite: 7]. An explicit clause in your main agreement (MSA) should explicitly say that all code, docs, and assets produced belong to your company as soon as they are written or paid for[cite: 7].</list_item>
      <list_item>Defined Jurisdiction: Don't leave legal jurisdiction up in the air[cite: 7]. Give the governing law its name and include international arbitration; otherwise, you will likely have no means to enforce the agreement across borders, making borderless hiring risky without legal clarity[cite: 7].</list_item>
    </section>

    <section>
      <sub_headline>Secure the Technology Platform</sub_headline>
      <body>Just a paper contract is a recipe for problems[cite: 7]. Technical controls make sure developers only have access to what they need to do their job—and nothing more[cite: 7].</body>
      <list_item>Limit Scope (Least Privilege): Consider using the principle of "Limit Scope (Least Privilege)" and only grant access to your monorepo to those developers who need it[cite: 7]. Break up services / keep front-end and back-end repositories separate to only show engineers the modules they are working on within your distributed engineering teams[cite: 7].</list_item>
      <list_item>Deploy Cloud Dev Environments: Rather than having engineers download raw code to their own, unencrypted laptops, deploy Cloud Dev Environments such as GitHub Codespaces or AWS Cloud9 via a VPN during remote developer onboarding[cite: 7]. The code remains in your cloud container and not in someone else's cloud drive[cite: 7].</list_item>
      <list_item>Keep Production Data Safe: Do not seed staging/local databases with live production data or real user info[cite: 7]. Build realistic schemas using synthetic data generators, without revealing customer records or sensitive metrics[cite: 7].</list_item>
    </section>

    <section>
      <sub_headline>Putting Your Daily Activities into Overdrive</sub_headline>
      <body>Operational discipline helps you make sure that your codebase remains clean, and that it doesn't slowly start leaking in the background[cite: 7].</body>
      <list_item>Audit Access Regularly: Maintain a central access log and regularly audit repo permissions, revoking access for contributors inactive during recent sprints[cite: 7].</list_item>
      <list_item>Automate Secret Scanning: Automate secret scanning with CI/CD validation rules that prevent commits containing API keys, passwords, or database URIs from merging[cite: 7].</list_item>
      <list_item>Follow a Strict Offboarding Checklist: Revoke identity provider, repository, and cloud credentials simultaneously upon contract termination, change accessed API keys/SSH keys, and clear temporary local files and cached builds[cite: 7].</list_item>
    </section>
