# Securing Your Code, Algorithms, and Key Technology Across Borders

Securing your code, algorithms, and key technology is as crucial as shipping features when you expand your dev team globally. It takes a combination of solid contracts and savvy, everyday security practices to get done. When scaling technical capacity across borders, you cannot rely solely on paper agreements or assume developers understand implicit security expectations. Building a resilient offshore operation requires an execution-level playbook that pairs enforceable legal frameworks with strict technical guardrails to maintain international tech compliance.

## Ensure Contracts Are in Place (Prior to Sharing Access)

Legal documents are not enough on their own to stop a bad actor, but they will put in place boundaries and, if there is a problem, the legal documents will give you teeth to bite with. Do not give access to the repository or system architecture information without signature:

- **Strict NDAs:** Ensure your NDA specifically includes product roadmaps, database schemas, binaries, and source code. It must also cover not only the prime agency/contractor, but any other people that they invite onto the project.
- **Work for Hire / Ownership Transfers:** An NDA obscures the facts, but does not necessarily mean you own the work. An explicit clause in your main agreement (MSA) should explicitly say that all code, docs, and assets produced belong to your company as soon as they are written or paid for.
- **Defined Jurisdiction:** Don't leave legal jurisdiction up in the air. Give the governing law its name and include international arbitration; otherwise, you will likely have no means to enforce the agreement across borders, making borderless hiring risky without legal clarity.

## Secure the Technology Platform

Just a paper contract is a recipe for problems. Technical controls make sure developers only have access to what they need to do their job—and nothing more.

- **Limit Scope (Least Privilege):** Consider using the principle of "Limit Scope (Least Privilege)" and only grant access to your monorepo to those developers who need it. Break up services / keep front-end and back-end repositories separate to only show engineers the modules they are working on within your distributed engineering teams.
- **Deploy Cloud Dev Environments:** Rather than having engineers download raw code to their own, unencrypted laptops, deploy Cloud Dev Environments such as GitHub Codespaces or AWS Cloud9 via a VPN during remote developer onboarding. The code remains in your cloud container and not in someone else's cloud drive.
- **Keep Production Data Safe:** Do not seed staging/local databases with live production data or real user info. Build realistic schemas using synthetic data generators, without revealing customer records or sensitive metrics.

## Putting Your Daily Activities into Overdrive

Operational discipline helps you make sure that your codebase remains clean, and that it doesn't slowly start leaking in the background.

- **Audit Access Regularly:** Maintain a central access log and regularly audit repo permissions, revoking access for contributors inactive during recent sprints.
- **Automate Secret Scanning:** Automate secret scanning with CI/CD validation rules that prevent commits containing API keys, passwords, or database URIs from merging.
- **Follow a Strict Offboarding Checklist:** Revoke identity provider, repository, and cloud credentials simultaneously upon contract termination, change accessed API keys/SSH keys, and clear temporary local files and cached builds.
