# Overcoming Time Zone Friction: Building a True Follow-the-Sun Product Roadmap

Employing people in distant time zones will almost always play out in one of two ways: Either there is a 24/7, clockwork product-development process, or you are stuck waiting 12 hours for one Slack response. To achieve the “follow the sun” model, which requires flawless work transition between continents, you need to remove the friction from daily activities.

## Stop Relying on Real-Time Meetings

A 7hr difference will make your team unproductive if everyone needs to be in the same Zoom meeting to make a decision.

- **Use Async:** All task, feature specs and bug reports must be well documented. Utilizing effective asynchronous work strategies, the task context should be very clear on the Nairobi ticket if a developer logs on as a developer logs off in San Francisco.
- **Establish and agree on Handoff Protocols:** Convene end-of-day updates. You keep the next time zone moving without delay with a simple three-line summary (What got shipped / What is blocked / What is next.).

## Take a Look at Overlap Hours, NOT Full Overlap

It is not necessary for team members to work on the night shift to remain connected. Try to overlap the 2–3-hour window as much as possible for your distributed engineering teams.

- That time is only to be used for unblocking, code review and high priority alignment.
- Limit deep-focus time to the non-overlapping hours.

## Focus on Output and Not Hours Online

There's no better way to mess with a global team than by having to see how their mouse moves; or when they're expected to be constantly replying to status updates.

- This may require setting up clear sprint output metrics, but as long as developers feel they are in control and have known expectations, geographic boundaries go out the window, fostering a healthy remote work culture and boosting remote talent retention.
